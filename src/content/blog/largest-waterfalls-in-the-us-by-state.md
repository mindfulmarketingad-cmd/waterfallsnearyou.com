  - q: "What is the tallest waterfall in the United States?"
    a: "A 2008 archived World Waterfall Database list of total height puts Oloʻupena Falls on Molokai, Hawaii, first among U.S. entries at 2,953 feet. Wikipedia cites that database and calls the figure unofficial. The tallest total a federal agency publishes in the sources used here is Yosemite Falls, at 2,425 feet."
  - q: "What does “largest” mean on this page?"
    a: "Largest by height means the greatest total vertical drop. Largest by volume means the most water going over the crest. Niagara is the volume fall. It is not in the height top ten."
  - q: "What is the largest U.S. waterfall by volume?"
    a: "Niagara Falls. A 1950 treaty with Canada, as summarized by the International Joint Commission, requires at least 100,000 cubic feet per second over the falls in the daytime tourist-season hours, and at least 50,000 cubic feet per second the rest of the year."
  - q: "Why do some states have no ranked list?"
    a: "A fall is ranked only when a USGS gazetteer sentence or a park-agency page states a height. Twenty-one of the 51 sections have no such sentence. Naming a tallest for them would mean inventing one."
  - q: "Why do “tallest east of the Rockies” claims disagree?"
    a: "They measure different things. Crabtree Falls at 1,200 feet is five cascades added together. Amicalola Falls at 729 feet is a cascade. Fall Creek Falls at 256 feet is one plunge."
  - q: "Are the Molokai sea-cliff falls open to hikers?"
    a: "No trail in our data reaches Oloʻupena, Puʻukaʻōkū, or Hāloku. ʻAkaka Falls, at 442 feet, is the tall Hawaii fall a state park builds a path to."
