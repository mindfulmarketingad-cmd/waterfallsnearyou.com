Raw waterfall counts answer which states have the most named falls. They do not tell you which states are packed with them, or which ones give each resident the most named falls to chase. For this Friday data study we took every named waterfall in our USGS-based directory, divided by Census land area and by 2020 Census population, and ranked all 50 states plus the District of Columbia both ways.

This is the companion to our earlier count ranking, [states with the most waterfalls](/blog/states-with-the-most-waterfalls). Same source data, different lens.

## Methodology

- **Waterfall counts:** Named features classified as Falls in the USGS Geographic Names Information System, as currently loaded on WaterfallsNearYou.com (2,644 listings across the 50 states and D.C. in this build).
- **Land area:** U.S. Census Bureau land area in square miles (excludes inland water).
- **Population:** 2020 Decennial Census resident population.
- **Density metrics:** `falls per 1,000 sq mi = named_falls / land_area_sq_mi × 1,000` and `falls per 1,000 residents = named_falls / (population / 1,000)`.
- **Scope:** 50 states and the District of Columbia. Territories are omitted here so area and population denominators match.
- **What this is not:** A ranking of beauty, height, flow or public access. It only measures how named Falls features are distributed relative to land and people.

National baseline for context: 2,644 named falls across 3,529,958 square miles of land and 330,459,333 residents works out to about 0.75 falls per 1,000 square miles and 0.0080 falls per 1,000 residents.

### What the area ranking shows

- **Hawaii is in a class by itself** at 17.44 named falls per 1,000 square miles, several times the next state.
- **Small northeastern states cluster near the top.** New Hampshire, Rhode Island, New York, Maine, Massachusetts and Vermont all clear 3 falls per 1,000 square miles.
- **Pacific Northwest leaders stay strong after adjusting for area.** Washington and Oregon both remain well above the national baseline.
- **Large arid or plains states drop.** Texas, Nevada and the Dakotas have vast land area relative to named Falls features, so their density ranks near the bottom even when absolute counts are not zero.

### What the population ranking shows

- **Wyoming and Montana lead** because sparse population magnifies mid-size waterfall inventories.
- **Maine and Hawaii appear near the top on both lists**, a rare combination of high areal density and high per-resident availability.
- **Big coastal states fall.** California and New York have large named-falls inventories, but tens of millions of residents pull their per-capita ranks down.
- **A high per-resident score is not a travel guide by itself.** Alaska ranks well per resident while remaining hard to reach; always check access, seasons and land ownership on the waterfall's page or its state list.
