Ithaca sits at the south end of Cayuga Lake with more public waterfalls in a short drive than almost any other small city in the Northeast. You can see a free city falls before breakfast, walk a state-park gorge before lunch, and stand under a 215-foot plunge by mid-afternoon without leaving Tompkins County. Stretch the day south to Seneca Lake and you add roadside falls and one of New York's most famous gorge walks.

This is the one-day loop I would drive first: start in town, climb through Robert H. Treman State Park, continue to Taughannock Falls, then decide whether you still have daylight for Montour Falls or Watkins Glen. Fees, dog rules and swimming limits below come from New York State Parks and the pages I've checked on this site; prices change, so confirm the week you go.

## The Route in Brief

1. **Ithaca Falls** on Lake Street for a free, five-minute stop.
2. **Lucifer Falls** at Robert H. Treman State Park, a short drive south of downtown.
3. **Taughannock Falls State Park** northwest of Ithaca on the west shore of Cayuga Lake.
4. Optional afternoon: **Shequaga Falls** in Montour Falls and/or **Watkins Glen State Park** on Seneca Lake.

Without the Seneca Lake side trip, plan four to six hours including short walks. With Watkins Glen's Gorge Trail, make it a full day and pack lunch.

## Ithaca Falls: Start in Town

[Ithaca Falls](/new-york/south-hill/ithaca-falls) drops Fall Creek over a wide ledge a few blocks from downtown. The viewing area is a short walk from Lake Street, with no state-park gate and no entrance fee. There is a city bus stop at the entrance, which makes this one of the easiest free waterfall stops in New York if you are staying in town without a car.

On Google it carries hundreds of reviews and a high average rating, which matches what you feel on site: a big, loud waterfall you can reach between coffee shops. Go early or on a weekday if you want the overlook without a crowd of cameras along the fence.

Treat it as a viewpoint, not a scramble. The rock around the creek is wet and the current is strong after rain. For trip details we have already verified, open the [Ithaca Falls page](/new-york/south-hill/ithaca-falls); it is also one of the free examples in our guide to [waterfall fees, permits and parking passes](/blog/waterfall-fees-permits-parking-passes).

Ten minutes here is enough. Then drive south toward Treman.

## Lucifer Falls at Robert H. Treman State Park

[Lucifer Falls](/new-york/south-hill/lucifer-falls) is the headline drop in Enfield Glen inside Robert H. Treman State Park. The park straddles Enfield Creek just south of Ithaca, with upper and lower entrances. From the upper area you can reach overlooks of Lucifer Falls; the gorge and rim trails connect the two ends of the park when conditions allow.

New York State Parks lists a **$10 per vehicle** entrance fee at Treman when fees are collected (typically the warmer months; confirm current collection hours with the park). The Empire Pass covers day-use entry at most state parks if you will hit Treman, Taughannock and Watkins Glen in the same trip. Dogs are allowed in day-use areas on a leash no longer than 6 feet, with a maximum of two pets, and are not allowed in the lifeguarded swimming area.

Swimming at Treman is in a designated, lifeguarded stream pool in season below the lower gorge—not at the base of Lucifer Falls. Stay on the trail in the gorge; algae on wet shale has caused serious falls across the Finger Lakes park system.

## Taughannock Falls: The Big Plunge

[Taughannock Falls](/new-york/trumansburg/taughannock-falls) plunges **215 feet** past cliffs that rise nearly 400 feet above the creek bed, according to New York State Parks. That makes it taller than Niagara's American Falls, though much narrower. The park sits in Trumansburg on Cayuga Lake, roughly a 15- to 20-minute drive northwest of downtown Ithaca.

The **Gorge Trail** is about 0.75 mile one way (about 1.5 miles round trip) on a wide, relatively flat path to the amphitheater at the base of the falls. Rim trails give top-down views if you prefer not to walk the gorge. As of April 17, 2026, the park reported all trails fully open; always call ahead after storms.

**Fees and rules** (from the park's official page, checked November 5, 2026):

| Detail | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicle fee | $10 per vehicle, collected daily sunup to sundown |
| Pass | Empire Pass accepted |
| Dogs | Leash ≤ 6 feet; max two pets in day-use areas; not on guarded beaches, playgrounds, boardwalks or in buildings |
| Swimming | Not in the gorge. Guarded Cayuga Lake beach in 2026: June 26–Labor Day, 11 a.m.–7 p.m. |
| Hours | Dawn to dusk year-round |

The Gorge Trail is one of the more accessible Finger Lakes waterfall walks because it stays relatively flat. Still, after heavy rain the surface can be muddy. Pack layers; the amphitheater stays cooler than the parking lot.

## Optional: Seneca Lake Side Trip

If you still have afternoon light, drive south toward Montour Falls and Watkins Glen on Seneca Lake. This adds roughly 45 minutes of driving each way from Ithaca depending on traffic, so decide before you leave Taughannock.

### Shequaga Falls (Montour Falls)

[Shequaga Falls](/new-york/montour-falls/shequaga-falls) (also called Montour Falls) drops beside the village in a small park setting. It is a short stop from the car and pairs well with a Seneca Lake lunch stop. Aunt Sarahs Falls is another short roadside cascade in the same village if you are collecting easy viewpoints.

### Hector Falls and Watkins Glen

[Hector Falls](/new-york/watkins-glen/hector-falls) is a multi-tier roadside waterfall south of Watkins Glen along the east side of Seneca Lake—useful as a quick photo stop rather than a hike. Watkins Glen State Park itself is the bigger draw: a stone-staircase gorge walk past many named cascades on Glen Creek. The park charges **$10 per vehicle**. **Dogs are not allowed on the Gorge Trail**; if you have a dog, use the rim trails and skip the gorge or leave the dog at home for that stop.

Watkins Glen crowds heavily in summer and fall-color weekends. Early morning is worth the alarm.

| Time | Stop | Time on site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8:30 a.m. | Ithaca Falls | 15 minutes |
| 9:00–11:00 a.m. | Robert H. Treman / Lucifer Falls | 1.5–2 hours |

In winter, shorten the list to Ithaca Falls and Taughannock's overlook, and treat gorge trails as ice hikes only if you have proper traction and the park has them open.

## Best Season for Finger Lakes Waterfalls

Finger Lakes gorges run on snowmelt and rain. Late winter and spring usually give the loudest falls. Summer is the season for combining a gorge walk with lake swimming at Taughannock or the pool at Treman, but expect more people and thinner cascades during dry spells. Fall color in the shale gorges is excellent, especially at Treman and Watkins Glen. Winter can be dramatic when ice builds on the cliffs, but trails may close or require microspikes.

For the broader seasonal pattern across the country, see [the best time to visit waterfalls](/blog/best-time-to-visit-waterfalls).

## Safety

Shale and wet stone in these gorges are slick even when they look dry. Stay on marked trails and behind railings. Do not climb toward the brink of Taughannock or Lucifer Falls for a photo. Gorge swimming outside designated, lifeguarded areas is a common cause of injuries in the region. After heavy rain, check park alerts for trail closures and high water.
