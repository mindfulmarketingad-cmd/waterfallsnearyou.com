Shenandoah National Park has some of the best waterfall hiking within a short drive of Washington, DC, and nearly all of it starts from a parking area on Skyline Drive. The catch is that almost every trail goes downhill first. You walk down a hollow to the falls and then climb back up to your car, so even short hikes here feel harder on the way out than the mileage suggests.

This guide lists the park's main waterfall hikes from north to south by Skyline Drive mile, the way the road is signed. Distances, climbs, difficulty ratings and pet rules come from the National Park Service's trail pages for each hike. If you're coming from the city, our page on [waterfalls near Washington, DC](/waterfalls-near/washington-dc) shows how these compare with closer options like the Great Falls of the Potomac.

## Before You Go

**You need an entrance pass.** Shenandoah charges an entrance fee whether you drive in on Skyline Drive or hike in from the boundary. A private vehicle pass is $30 and covers everyone in the car for seven consecutive days. Walk-in and bicycle visitors 16 and older pay $15 each, and an annual Shenandoah pass is $55. The park is cashless at its entrance stations, so bring a card. The America the Beautiful pass covers entry too. Our guide to [waterfall fees, permits and parking passes](/blog/waterfall-fees-permits-parking-passes) explains how these passes work at other parks and forests.

**Plan for the climb back.** The Park Service lists elevation gain for every hike below, and on most of them nearly all of that gain comes at the end. A 1.4-mile hike with 440 feet of climbing is a very different outing from a flat 1.4-mile walk. Leave more time than the posted hiking time if you have kids or anyone who doesn't hike often.

**Check the dog rules.** Leashed pets (6-foot leash or shorter) are allowed on most Shenandoah trails, including most of the waterfall hikes here. Dark Hollow Falls is the main exception.

**Wet rock is the real hazard.** Every one of these falls has slick, algae-covered rock around it. The Park Service notes that the Dark Hollow trail is very steep and rocky and asks hikers to take their time when it's wet. Our [waterfall safety tips](/blog/waterfall-safety-tips) cover the habits that prevent most falls.

## North District

### Overall Run Falls (Mile 22.2)

[Overall Run Falls](/virginia/washington/overall-run-falls) is the tallest waterfall in the park at 93 feet, and it's the longest hike on this list. The route starts at the Mathews Arm Campground registration parking area and follows Traces Trail to the yellow-blazed Mathews Arm Road, which turns into Mathews Arm Trail. At the Tuscarora-Overall Run Trail, turn left. About a tenth of a mile on, a side trail looks at the upper falls, and another 1,300 feet downhill brings you to views of the main drop.

It's 5.1 miles round trip with 1,291 feet of gain, and the Park Service rates it Moderately Strenuous. When the campground is closed for the season, you can park at mile 21 and take the Appalachian Trail to the Tuscarora-Overall Run Trail, which adds about a mile.

Timing matters more here than anywhere else in the park. The Park Service says that unless rain has been plentiful, Overall Run may have minimal to no water. Save it for spring or the days after a big storm. The Park Service also has closures in effect on Overall Run to keep the rock outcrop ecosystems there from being degraded further, so stay on the trail and behind any posted closures.

## Central District

The Central District, between Thornton Gap and Swift Run Gap, is where most of Shenandoah's waterfall hiking is. Four of the hikes in this guide start within about nine miles of each other, close to Skyland and Big Meadows, so it's easy to do two in a day.

### Whiteoak Canyon (Mile 42.6 and the Route 600 Boundary)

Whiteoak Canyon has been a popular hike since the early days of Skyland Resort, and you can approach it from the top or the bottom.

From the top, [Upper Whiteoak Falls](/virginia/stanley/upper-whiteoak-falls) is 4.6 miles round trip from the Whiteoak Canyon parking area at mile 42.6, with 1,040 feet of gain. You pass the junctions for the Old Rag Fire Road and the Limberlost Trail, and about 2.3 miles in you reach a rocky ledge with a view of the upper falls. Then you turn around and climb back out.

From the bottom, [Lower Whiteoak Falls](/virginia/stanley/lower-whiteoak-falls) is the gentlest waterfall hike in the park. It starts at the Whiteoak Canyon boundary parking area at the end of Route 600, outside Skyline Drive, and runs 2 miles round trip with 500 feet of gain to the base of the lower falls and cascades. It's the only waterfall hike here that the Park Service rates Easiest. You still need a park pass from this side, and passes are sold at the lower trailhead.

Strong hikers can link the two sides. The Park Service's Cedar Run and Whiteoak circuit is 8.1 miles with 2,794 feet of climbing, so treat it as a full day.

### Rose River Falls (Mile 49.4)

[Rose River Falls](/virginia/stanley/rose-river-falls) drops 67 feet in the Shenandoah Wilderness. The Park Service route is a 4-mile loop from the Fishers Gap parking area with 910 feet of gain. Cross Skyline Drive, start down the fire road, turn left onto the yellow-blazed Skyland-Big Meadows Horse Trail, and after about 0.6 mile pick up the blue-blazed Rose River Loop Trail down past the falls. The return climbs a steep, rocky section past more cascades to the fire road.

This is the dog-friendly alternative to Dark Hollow. It also lets you add Dark Hollow Falls: from the fire road near the end of the loop, a short side trip of about a quarter mile takes you down to the base of Dark Hollow Falls and back.

### Dark Hollow Falls (Mile 50.7)

[Dark Hollow Falls](/virginia/stanley/dark-hollow-falls) is one of the most traveled trails in the park, and it's the busiest waterfall on this site's list for Shenandoah, with more Google reviews than any other falls in the park. The hike follows a stream steeply down 0.75 mile from the Dark Hollow Falls parking area, then comes back the same way. It's 1.4 miles round trip with 440 feet of gain, and the Park Service rates it Moderate and allows about an hour and 15 minutes.

Two things catch people out. First, pets are not allowed on this trail. Second, the trail is short but very steep and rocky, and the Park Service specifically warns that the return climb is challenging. If someone in your group isn't sure about the climb, Lower Whiteoak is the easier choice.

The parking area fills on fall weekends. Arriving early helps, and so does pairing Dark Hollow with the Rose River loop from Fishers Gap, a little over a mile north.

### Lewis Falls (Mile 51.2)

Lewis Falls is an 81-foot waterfall reached from Big Meadows. The trail starts behind the amphitheater in the back of the Big Meadows picnic grounds. Walk downhill from the concrete marker post, turn left on the Appalachian Trail, then bear right onto the blue-blazed Lewis Falls Trail for a steep, rocky descent to a viewing platform. The return climbs to a fire road and rejoins the Appalachian Trail back to the start.

The loop is 3.3 miles with 990 feet of gain, and the Park Service allows about four hours. Because it starts at Big Meadows, it's a good choice if you're staying at the lodge or campground there. Leashed dogs are allowed.

### South River Falls (Mile 62.7)

[South River Falls](/virginia/stanardsville/south-river-falls) is 83 feet tall and sits at the southern end of the Central District. The hike starts just below the comfort station at the South River Picnic Grounds and follows the blue-blazed South River Falls Trail, crossing the Appalachian Trail, to a stone-walled observation point. The Park Service route continues past the overlook and returns on South River Falls Road, the South River Fire Road and the Appalachian Trail, making a 3.3-mile circuit with 910 feet of gain and one stream crossing.

The Park Service calls it a great hike for wildflower and bird lovers. It's also usually quieter than the Skyland and Big Meadows hikes because it's farther from the busiest stretch of the road.

## South District

### Doyles River Falls (Mile 81.1)

[Doyles River Falls](/virginia/grottoes/doyles-river-falls) is really two waterfalls. From the Doyles River parking area at mile 81.1, near Loft Mountain, the blue-blazed Doyles River Trail crosses the Appalachian Trail, passes the access trails to Doyles River Cabin and Browns Gap Road, and reaches trail posts marking the Upper and Lower Doyles River Falls. It's 3.3 miles round trip with 1,189 feet of gain, rated Moderate, with a stream crossing.

Jones Run Falls is nearby in the South District. The Park Service lists it as a 3.2-mile round trip with 1,045 feet of gain, so it's a similar effort and a good second hike if you're camping at Loft Mountain.

## Picking the Right Hike

If you only have one morning, here's how I'd choose:

- **Shortest walk:** Dark Hollow Falls, if everyone can handle a steep climb and you've left the dog at home.
- **Easiest grade:** Lower Whiteoak Falls from the Route 600 boundary lot.
- **With a dog:** Rose River Falls, the loop the Park Service itself recommends for dog owners.
- **Biggest waterfall:** Overall Run Falls, but only after rain.
- **Fewer people:** South River Falls or Doyles River Falls.
- **Staying at Big Meadows:** Lewis Falls straight from the campground area.

Families should look at the elevation numbers before the distance. Our list of [waterfall hikes with kids](/blog/waterfall-hikes-with-kids) has more tips for keeping a short hike fun when the hard part is at the end.

## Best Time to Visit

Shenandoah's waterfalls are fed by small mountain streams, so flow depends heavily on recent rain. Spring is the most reliable season, with full streams and wildflowers along the trails. October brings fall color and the park's biggest crowds. In summer and early fall, check the weather and aim for a day or two after a good rain, especially for Overall Run.

Winter can be beautiful, with ice along the falls, but the trails can be icy and sections of Skyline Drive close in bad weather. Check the park's road status before you drive. Our guide to the [best time to visit waterfalls](/blog/best-time-to-visit-waterfalls) explains how season and rain affect flow more generally.

## Combining Shenandoah With the Blue Ridge Parkway

Skyline Drive ends at Rockfish Gap in the south, where the Blue Ridge Parkway begins. If you have more than a weekend, you can keep driving south to Apple Orchard Falls near Peaks of Otter and on into North Carolina, where waterfalls are much more frequent along the road. ## A Few Practical Notes

- Bring water even on short hikes. The climb out is the hardest part of most of these trails, and it often comes in the warmest part of the day.
- Wear shoes with real tread. Rose River, Lewis Falls, South River and Doyles River all include rocky sections or stream crossings.
- Download the trail map from the Park Service page for your hike before you lose cell signal on Skyline Drive.
- Respect closures at Overall Run and anywhere else the park has fenced off rock outcrops or eroded areas.
- Pack out everything you bring in. Our [Leave No Trace guide for waterfalls](/blog/leave-no-trace-at-waterfalls) covers the details.

Fees and closures change, so confirm them the week you go.
